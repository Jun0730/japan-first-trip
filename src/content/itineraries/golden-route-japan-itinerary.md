The "Golden Route" connects Japan's three most-visited cities by shinkansen and is the most common first-timer
itinerary shape for a reason — it covers a genuinely wide range of what draws people to Japan without requiring
complex routing. The exact number of days is flexible; adjust the split below to match your actual trip length.

## Tokyo: modern city, food, and day trips

Tokyo works well as either your first or last stop, since it's the most connected to international flights via
Narita and Haneda. Spend enough time here to cover a few distinct neighborhoods (see our
[Tokyo transportation guide](/transportation/how-to-buy-jr-pass/) for getting around) rather than trying to see
all of Tokyo in a rush — it rewards a slower pace more than a checklist approach.

## Kyoto: temples, traditional culture, and day trips to Nara

Kyoto is the traditional-culture core of the route — temples, historic districts, and easy day trips to
[Nara](/guides/nara/). Most first-time visitors find 3-4 days enough to see the highlights without feeling
rushed; see our [3-day Kyoto itinerary](/itineraries/3-day-kyoto-highlights/) for a tighter version if your
overall trip is shorter.

## Osaka: food and a shorter, energetic stop

Osaka is often treated as the shortest stop on the route, anchored around food culture (Dotonbori) and a more
casual, energetic pace than Kyoto. It also works as a base for day trips if you want to add Nara or Kobe without
moving hotels.

## Connecting the cities

All three cities connect by shinkansen, with Kyoto and Osaka close enough together (under an hour) that some
travelers base themselves in one and day-trip to the other rather than changing hotels for just those two
stops. Whether the [Japan Rail Pass](/blog/jr-pass-calculator/) is worth it for this route depends on how many
long-distance legs you're taking — check the math for your specific plan rather than assuming it's automatic.

## Where to stay

Shinjuku or [Tokyo Station](/where-to-stay/) work well as a Tokyo base, and
[Kyoto Station](/where-to-stay/kyoto-station-area/) remains the most convenient single base for the Kyoto/Osaka
portion of the trip if you don't want to change hotels for a short Osaka stay.

## Bottom line

The Golden Route's appeal is that it's hard to get wrong — three major cities, well-connected by fast trains,
each offering a distinctly different experience. Adjust the day split above based on your total trip length
rather than treating 10 days as a fixed requirement.
