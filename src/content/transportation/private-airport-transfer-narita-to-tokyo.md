Narita Express (N'EX) or the Skyliner are the default recommendation for getting into Tokyo, and for a single
traveler with light luggage, they're usually the cheaper option. A private transfer makes more sense in a
specific set of situations.

## When a private transfer is worth it

- **You're arriving late at night**, after last train connections, or landing exhausted after a long-haul
  flight and don't want to navigate transfers and platform changes.
- **You're traveling with 3 or more people**, where the per-vehicle price starts to compete with (or beat) the
  combined cost of individual train tickets.
- **You have a lot of luggage** — oversized suitcases, sports equipment, or traveling with young kids and a
  stroller — where hauling bags through station transfers is more hassle than it's worth.
- **Your hotel isn't near a train station**, meaning you'd need a taxi from the station anyway on top of the
  train fare.

## What to expect

A private transfer is typically booked in advance, with a driver meeting you at the arrivals area (sometimes
with a name sign, depending on the operator) and taking you directly to your hotel or accommodation address —
no transfers, no navigating with luggage through a station. Journey time into central Tokyo depends heavily on
traffic and is often comparable to or slower than the train, so this is a comfort and convenience trade, not
usually a speed one.

## Before you book

Confirm what's included — some transfer bookings are for a specific vehicle size with a luggage limit, so if
you're traveling as a larger group or with unusually bulky luggage, check the listing's capacity before
assuming it'll fit everyone and everything in one trip.

## Bottom line

For a solo or two-person trip with normal luggage, the train is usually still the better value. For a tired
late-night arrival, a larger group, or heavy luggage, a private transfer is worth the price difference for the
convenience alone.
