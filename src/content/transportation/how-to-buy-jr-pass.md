Since the Japan Rail Pass moved to digital passes in most cases, the buying process is simpler than the older
paper exchange-order system, but there are still a few decision points worth knowing before you order.

## The general process

Passes are generally bought online before your trip, either as a digital pass activated through an app or
linked to your passport for gate scanning, or in some cases still as an exchange order redeemed at a JR office
in Japan. Which options are available can change, so check current purchase methods before assuming either
system applies.

## What to have ready before ordering

- **Your passport details** — passes are tied to your passport as a temporary visitor, and mismatched details
  can cause issues at activation.
- **Your planned start date** — most passes activate on a specific date you choose at purchase or redemption,
  not automatically from the purchase date, so pick a date that matches when your rail travel actually begins.
- **Confirmation of your specific itinerary's rail needs** — buy the pass only after roughly mapping out which
  long-distance legs you'll actually take, since the pass isn't automatically the best value for every itinerary
  (see our [JR Pass calculator guide](/blog/jr-pass-calculator/) for how to check this).

## Common mistakes when buying

- **Buying before confirming it's actually worth it for your route.** The pass has been through price increases
  in recent years, which has narrowed the itineraries where it clearly beats buying individual tickets.
- **Choosing an activation date that doesn't match your actual first long-distance train day**, wasting valid
  days at the start of the pass window.
- **Not checking seat reservation requirements** — some trains and routes require a reservation even with a
  valid pass, which isn't automatic in every booking flow.

## Bottom line

Buy your Japan Rail Pass only after confirming it's worth it for your specific itinerary, have your passport
details and intended start date ready, and check current purchase/activation methods before assuming an older
process still applies.
