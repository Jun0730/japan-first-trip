Fushimi Inari Shrine is free to enter and open around the clock, so you don't strictly need a tour to visit —
but a guide adds context you'd otherwise miss: the meaning behind the torii gate donations, which sub-shrines
are worth a detour, and how far up the mountain trail is actually worth walking.

## Why go early

By mid-morning, the lower section of the torii tunnel near the entrance gets crowded with tour groups. Early
tours typically start before sunrise or right at opening, when the path is quiet enough to actually see the
gates without a line of people in every photo.

## What's usually included

Most small-group tours cover the main torii tunnel and at least one viewpoint partway up the mountain, with a
guide explaining the shrine's history and Shinto fox (kitsune) symbolism along the way. Check the specific
tour's listing for exact route, duration and whether breakfast is included — details vary by operator and
change over time.

## Good to know

Wear comfortable shoes — even the shorter routes involve stairs and uneven stone paths. The full loop to the
summit and back can take two or more hours round trip, so confirm how far your specific tour goes if you have a
tight morning schedule.
