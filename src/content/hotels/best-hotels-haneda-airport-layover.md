Whether a layover hotel makes sense at Haneda depends almost entirely on your layover length and what time of
day it falls.

## How long is your layover?

- **Under 6 hours:** Usually not worth leaving the terminal at all. An hourly-rate capsule or lounge inside the
  terminal (Haneda has both) makes more sense than a hotel commute.
- **6-12 hours, especially overnight:** An in-terminal or airport-adjacent hotel is worth it — enough time to
  actually sleep, but not enough to justify a trip into central Tokyo and back.
- **12+ hours:** Worth considering a hotel in central Tokyo instead, since Haneda's train connections into the
  city are fast enough that "airport hotel vs. a real hotel with a quick train ride" becomes a genuine choice.

## In-terminal vs. airport-adjacent

Haneda has capsule-style sleeping pods and lounges directly inside the terminal for short rests without going
through immigration again (relevant if you haven't cleared it yet). Airport-adjacent hotels outside the
terminal offer full rooms and are usually reachable by a short shuttle, at the cost of needing to clear
security again on the way back.

## What to check before booking

- **Whether you've cleared immigration yet** — in-terminal international-side options only work before you
  clear immigration; once you're through, you're in the same position as any other Tokyo-area traveler.
- **Shuttle schedule and frequency** for airport-adjacent properties, especially for very early or late flights.
- **Whether the room is booked in local time or a block of hours** — some in-terminal options are sold in fixed
  time blocks rather than a standard overnight booking.

## Bottom line

For a short layover, stay in the terminal. For a longer overnight layover, an airport-adjacent hotel is the
simpler choice. For a genuinely long layover, Haneda's fast rail connections make central Tokyo a real option
worth comparing against staying near the airport.
