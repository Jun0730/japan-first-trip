"Best family hotel" lists often just rank generically well-reviewed hotels, which misses what actually matters
for traveling with kids in Tokyo specifically: room size, being near a station without stairs-only access, and
proximity to family-friendly food options.

## Room size matters more in Tokyo than almost anywhere else

Standard Tokyo hotel rooms are often built for one or two adults, not a family of four. Check the actual square
meterage (usually listed) rather than assuming "family room" means spacious by international standards — it
often just means one extra bed squeezed into a similarly-sized room.

## Location: prioritize step-free station access

Not every Tokyo station has elevator access to every platform or exit, which matters a lot with a stroller or
tired kids and luggage. Check accessibility for your specific station and hotel before booking, especially if
you'll be moving between hotels or doing day trips with a stroller.

## What else to check

- **Proximity to a convenience store or supermarket** — useful for snacks, basic supplies, and kid-friendly food
  options at odd hours, which is a bigger convenience with kids than without.
- **Laundry facilities**, either in-room or in the building — genuinely useful for longer family trips to avoid
  overpacking.
- **Breakfast inclusion and kid-friendly options** — not all Tokyo hotel breakfasts are set up for picky eaters;
  check reviews for specifics if this matters to you.

## Areas worth considering

Shinjuku, Tokyo Station, and Asakusa areas all combine good step-free transit access with a wide range of
nearby food options — worth prioritizing over a "prettier" but less accessible neighborhood if traveling with
young kids.

## Bottom line

For a family stay in Tokyo, checking actual room dimensions and step-free station access matters more than
generic hotel ratings. A slightly lower-rated hotel with a genuinely spacious room and easy stroller access will
usually serve a family better than a highly-rated but cramped one.
