Capsule hotels have gone from a novelty to a genuinely practical budget option in Shinjuku, one of Tokyo's best-
connected areas. They're not for everyone, but for the right traveler they solve a real problem: cheap,
central, and clean.

## What a capsule stay actually involves

You get an individual sleeping pod (not a full room) with a curtain or door for privacy, usually within a
shared-bathroom facility that includes lockers for luggage. Quality varies a lot between properties — some are
closer to a design hotel experience, others are more purely functional. Check recent photos and reviews for the
specific property rather than assuming all capsule hotels are the same standard.

## Who this suits

- **Solo travelers** comfortable with shared facilities and prioritizing location and price over space.
- **Late-night arrivals or early departures**, since Shinjuku's transit connections make getting to/from the
  airport or other cities simple regardless of what time you land.
- **Short stopovers** where you mainly need somewhere to sleep, shower, and store luggage rather than a room to
  spend the day in.

## Who should look elsewhere

Traveling as a couple or family, need to work from the room, or want space to unpack for a longer stay — a
standard hotel room will serve you much better than a capsule, even at a higher price.

## What to check before booking

- **Luggage storage size** — capsule hotels vary in how large a suitcase their lockers can fit; check this if
  traveling with more than a carry-on.
- **Gender-separated floors or female-only properties**, which many capsule hotels offer — worth checking if
  that matters to you.
- **Curfew or re-entry policies** — some properties lock check-in/out to specific hours.

## Bottom line

A well-reviewed capsule hotel in Shinjuku is a legitimate way to cut accommodation cost without sacrificing
location, provided you're traveling solo or don't mind shared facilities. For couples, families, or longer
stays, it's worth comparing against a small standard room instead.
