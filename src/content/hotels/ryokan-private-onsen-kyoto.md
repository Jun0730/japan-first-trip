A ryokan stay is as much about the format — tatami rooms, multi-course kaiseki dinner, yukata robes — as it is
about the onsen itself. A private onsen room adds a specific benefit on top of that: bathing without sharing a
space with strangers.

## Private vs. shared onsen: what actually changes

A shared (communal) onsen is bathed nude, typically gender-separated, following etiquette like washing before
entering the bath. A private onsen room — either an in-room bath or a bookable private bathing time slot —
removes the social aspect entirely, which matters if you have tattoos (many shared onsens restrict or ban
visible tattoos), are traveling as a couple who want to bathe together, or are simply not comfortable with
communal nude bathing.

## What's usually included

Most ryokan bookings, private-onsen or not, include a multi-course kaiseki dinner and traditional breakfast as
part of the room rate — factor this into any price comparison against a standard hotel, since it's effectively
including two meals a standard hotel room wouldn't. Confirm meal inclusion on the specific listing, since some
budget-oriented ryokan sell room-only rates.

## What to check before booking

- **Whether the "private onsen" is an in-room bath or a bookable shared-but-private time slot** — these are
  different setups with different room configurations.
- **Dinner timing and dietary restrictions** — kaiseki dinners are often served at a fixed time and can be
  difficult to modify for allergies or dietary needs; flag this when booking, not on arrival.
- **Check-in/dinner timing against your day's itinerary** — ryokan dinners are often earlier and more fixed in
  timing than a hotel restaurant, which can affect how you plan the rest of your day.

## Bottom line

A private onsen ryokan is worth the premium over a standard hotel if the ryokan experience itself (kaiseki
dinner, tatami rooms, the ritual of it) is part of what you want from the trip, or if a communal onsen isn't a
comfortable option for you. If you just want a nice hotel room, a standard property will be considerably
cheaper for a comparable standard of room.
