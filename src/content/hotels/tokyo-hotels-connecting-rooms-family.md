Tokyo hotel rooms run smaller on average than many Western travelers expect, and true connecting rooms (two
separate rooms with an internal connecting door) are a limited inventory item at most properties — not every
hotel offers them, and even ones that do may only have a handful.

## Why this is harder to book than it sounds

Connecting rooms are typically a specific, limited room type rather than something assignable at check-in on
request. Booking a "family room" or two standard rooms without confirming they connect doesn't guarantee
they'll be adjacent, let alone actually connected. If a connecting room matters to your trip, it needs to be
booked (and ideally confirmed) as that specific room type in advance.

## How to actually secure one

- **Search or filter specifically for "connecting rooms"** rather than "family rooms," which is a different
  (usually just larger) room category that may not connect to anything.
- **Contact the property directly after booking** to confirm the connecting room type is what's been reserved,
  especially for larger hotels with mixed room configurations.
- **Book well ahead of your trip** — connecting rooms are a small fraction of most hotels' inventory and sell
  out earlier than standard rooms.

## Alternatives if you can't find one

- **A single larger family room**, which some hotels offer as a bigger space for up to 4-5 people instead of
  two connected rooms.
- **An apartment-style stay**, which more reliably offers multiple bedrooms under one unit than hotel connecting
  rooms do, at the cost of hotel amenities like daily housekeeping.

## Bottom line

Don't assume any two rooms booked together will connect — search specifically for the connecting-room type,
book early, and confirm directly with the property if it matters to your trip. If none are available for your
dates, a family room or apartment-style stay are the more reliable fallbacks.
