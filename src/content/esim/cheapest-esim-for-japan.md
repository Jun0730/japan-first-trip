"Cheapest eSIM for Japan" searches usually turn up whichever plan has the lowest sticker price, which isn't
the same thing as the cheapest plan for your actual trip. A tiny data allowance that runs out on day 3 forces a
second purchase, which usually costs more in total than buying the right size once.

## Compare price per GB, not price per plan

Data plans are rarely priced linearly — a slightly bigger tier is often only a little more expensive than the
smallest one, which makes the price-per-GB much better. Before buying the cheapest-looking option, check what a
mid-tier plan costs and how much more data it includes; the jump in price is often smaller than the jump in
data.

## Why pay-as-you-go tiers tend to be the actual cheapest option

Providers like Airalo sell fixed data tiers rather than unlimited plans, which means you're not paying a
premium for data you won't use — a real advantage for budget-conscious short trips where you know roughly how
much you'll need. Unlimited plans (like Holafly's) can be cheaper per day for heavy users, but for light-to-
moderate use over a week or less, a correctly-sized tiered plan is usually the lower total cost.

## Other ways people overspend on eSIMs without realizing it

- **Buying a global/regional plan when a Japan-only plan is cheaper.** Regional Asia plans are convenient if
  you're visiting multiple countries, but if Japan is your only stop, a Japan-specific plan is usually priced
  lower for the same data amount.
- **Not checking if your phone actually needs an eSIM at all.** If you're only in Japan a few days and mostly
  rely on hotel/cafe Wi-Fi plus offline maps, a cheap eSIM tier or even a free airport Wi-Fi service might cover
  you without needing a bigger plan.
- **Ignoring hotspot/tethering restrictions.** Some cheaper tiers restrict or disable hotspot sharing — check
  this before assuming you can share the connection across your travel group's devices.

## Bottom line

The cheapest eSIM for a Japan trip is usually a correctly-sized, Japan-specific, pay-as-you-go tier rather than
the plan with the lowest listed price. Check price per GB across two or three tiers before deciding, and only
consider an unlimited plan if you know your usage will be genuinely heavy.
