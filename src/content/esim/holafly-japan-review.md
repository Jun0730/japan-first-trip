Holafly's pitch is simple: pay a flat price for a fixed number of days and get unlimited data for the whole
trip, no tiers to calculate. Whether that's actually a good deal for you depends almost entirely on how much
data you'd use per day if you weren't worried about running out.

## Who unlimited data actually benefits

If you plan to stream video or music regularly, do a lot of video calls, tether other devices, or simply don't
want to think about data usage at all during the trip, unlimited removes that mental overhead entirely. Heavy
users often come out ahead on both cost and convenience compared to buying and re-buying tiered plans.

## Who it doesn't benefit

Light users — people mostly using maps, messaging apps, and occasional browsing — often pay more with an
unlimited plan than they would with a correctly-sized tiered plan from a provider like Airalo or a Japan-
specialist like Sakura Mobile. Before buying, it's worth roughly estimating your daily data use and comparing
that against what a tiered plan of similar length would cost.

## Known trade-offs with unlimited eSIM plans generally

- **Speed throttling after a threshold** is common across the unlimited-eSIM industry (not unique to any one
  provider) — "unlimited" doesn't always mean unlimited-at-full-speed indefinitely. Check the current terms
  before assuming otherwise.
- **Hotspot/tethering support varies by plan** — confirm this if you're planning to share the connection across
  multiple devices.
- **You're paying for days, not usage** — if your plans change and you use the eSIM for fewer days than
  purchased, you don't get a partial refund on unused days.

## Bottom line

Holafly (and unlimited eSIM plans generally) make the most sense for genuinely heavy data users who want to
stop thinking about usage entirely. For a lighter, typical first-timer usage pattern — maps, messaging,
occasional social posts — a tiered plan sized to your actual trip is usually the better value.
