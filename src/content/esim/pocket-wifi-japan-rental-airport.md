Pocket WiFi rental counters exist at Japan's major airports, but showing up without a reservation means relying
on same-day stock and paying walk-up pricing. Pre-booking online before you fly is simpler and typically
cheaper.

## How airport pickup usually works

You reserve online before your trip, then collect the physical device at a counter in the arrivals area (exact
counter location depends on the airport and provider — check your booking confirmation). Return is usually just
as simple: drop the unit in a return box before departure, or use a prepaid return envelope by mail, depending
on the service.

## Why pre-booking beats walk-up rental

Walk-up counters can run out of units during busy travel periods, and same-day pricing is rarely the best rate
available. Booking a Japan-specialist service like Sakura Mobile ahead of time secures a unit and typically
locks in better pricing than whatever's available same-day at the counter.

## What to check before booking

- **How many devices need to connect.** Confirm the unit's simultaneous-device limit if you're sharing across a
  family or group.
- **Battery life for your typical day.** Most rental units last a full day of moderate use, but heavy
  simultaneous use across several devices will drain it faster — carrying a portable charger is a reasonable
  backup.
- **Pickup and return logistics for your specific airport and flight times**, especially for very early or late
  flights when counter staffing may be reduced.

## Bottom line

If you're traveling with others and want to share one connection, pre-booking pocket WiFi pickup at the airport
with a Japan-focused provider is simpler and usually cheaper than relying on same-day walk-up rental.
