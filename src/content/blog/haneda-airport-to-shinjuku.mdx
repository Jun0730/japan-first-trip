Haneda's proximity to central Tokyo means there are several reasonable ways to reach Shinjuku, and the "best"
one depends more on your luggage and arrival time than on raw speed, since all three main options are
reasonably fast.

## Train (Keikyu/Tokyo Monorail + transfer)

The cheapest option, involving at least one transfer to reach Shinjuku directly. Works well for light luggage
and daytime arrivals when stations are staffed and less crowded. Less practical late at night when transfer
frequency drops, or with a lot of luggage given station stairs and crowded platforms.

## Airport Limousine Bus

A direct bus route to Shinjuku (and other major hub areas) without transfers, with luggage stored underneath
rather than carried through stations. Costs more than the train but meaningfully less hassle with bags, and
runs on a fixed schedule you can plan around in advance.

## Private Transfer or Taxi

The most expensive per person for a solo traveler, but priced per vehicle rather than per person — for 3 or
more people traveling together, or very early/late arrivals when trains and buses run less frequently, this can
end up cost-competitive while being the least effort.

## How to decide

- **Light luggage, daytime arrival, traveling solo or as a pair:** the train is the cheapest and perfectly
  practical option.
- **Heavier luggage, don't want to deal with transfers:** the limousine bus is the middle-ground choice.
- **Late-night arrival, large group, or lots of luggage:** a private transfer or taxi removes the most friction,
  and the per-vehicle pricing narrows the cost gap for groups.

## Bottom line

For most solo and light-luggage travelers, the train is the practical default. Groups, heavy luggage, or
late-night arrivals are where the bus or a private transfer earns its higher price.
