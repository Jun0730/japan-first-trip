Kansai sightseeing means a lot of walking and a fair amount of train transfers. This list focuses on what
actually matters for that, not a generic every-destination packing list.

## Footwear

Comfortable, broken-in walking shoes matter more than almost anything else on this list — expect to walk
several miles most days between temples, markets and train stations. Slip-on shoes are genuinely useful, since
some temples and traditional restaurants require removing your shoes at the entrance.

## Layers, not a single heavy jacket

Kansai's weather swings meaningfully by season (see our [best time to visit guide](/blog/best-time-to-visit-kansai/)),
and even within a single day, temperature can shift enough that layers work better than one heavy layer.

## A portable charger

Between navigation apps, translation apps and photos, phone battery drains faster while traveling. A compact
power bank is worth the bag space.

## Cash

Japan is more cash-friendly than many first-time visitors expect. While cards are increasingly accepted, many
smaller restaurants, temples and market stalls are cash-only. Carrying some cash, and knowing where ATMs that
accept foreign cards are (convenience store ATMs are generally reliable), saves friction.

## A day bag for temple visits

Something you can carry hands-free (backpack or crossbody bag) makes it easier to manage shoes-on/shoes-off
temple etiquette, tickets, and the inevitable souvenirs you'll pick up along the way.

## What you probably don't need

A universal adapter with voltage conversion is usually unnecessary — Japan uses a two-pin plug similar to North
America, so most North American travelers won't need a converter, only a plug shape adapter if their devices
use a different plug style. Double-check your specific devices before assuming.
