The Japan Rail Pass used to be an almost automatic recommendation for any multi-city trip. After price
increases in recent years, it's worth actually checking the math for your specific route rather than assuming
it's the better deal.

## The simple version of the calculation

List every long-distance train leg your itinerary actually includes (shinkansen between major cities, not
local subway or bus trips, which the pass doesn't meaningfully help with), look up the current individual
ticket price for each leg, and add them up. Compare that total against the current pass price for the number
of days you'd need. If the individual tickets add up to less than the pass, buy tickets individually instead.

## What skews the calculation

- **Round trips between two distant cities** (e.g., Tokyo to Kyoto and back) push the math toward the pass being
  worth it, since that's exactly the kind of long-distance travel it's priced around.
- **Itineraries that stay mostly within one region** (like Kansai-only trips) push the math against the pass,
  since local and regional trains don't benefit from it as much as long-distance shinkansen legs.
- **Very short trips with only one long-distance leg** often don't reach the pass's break-even point at all —
  it tends to pay off more clearly on 3+ long-distance legs.

## Don't forget these when comparing

- **Regional passes** covering a single area (like a Kansai-specific pass) are sometimes a better fit than the
  nationwide pass if your trip doesn't leave one region.
- **Seat reservations** are sometimes required regardless of pass ownership — this doesn't change the cost
  comparison but is a step people forget to plan for.
- Ticket and pass prices are updated periodically — always check current prices for your calculation rather
  than relying on older figures, including the ones in this article.

## Bottom line

The Japan Rail Pass is still worth it for itineraries built around several long-distance shinkansen legs, but
no longer an automatic default for every multi-city trip. Do the simple leg-by-leg comparison for your specific
route before buying.
