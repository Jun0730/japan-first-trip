Suica and Pasmo are competing IC cards issued by different operators (JR East and a consortium of private/metro
railways, respectively), but for a tourist just tapping in and out of trains and buses, the practical
difference between the two is close to zero.

## Why the brand barely matters for tourists

Both cards work identically across nearly all trains, subways, and buses in the Tokyo area and most of the rest
of the country, and both work the same way at convenience stores and vending machines for small purchases.
Neither card unlocks routes or discounts the other doesn't — the choice mostly comes down to which one is
available and easiest to get where you are.

## What actually does matter

- **Physical card vs. phone-based version**: if your phone supports it, adding Suica or Pasmo to your phone's
  wallet app skips carrying a physical card entirely — this is a bigger practical difference than which brand
  you pick.
- **Where you're buying it**: pick up whichever is more conveniently available at your arrival station rather
  than seeking out a specific brand — availability has varied at different times for foreign-issued cards, so
  check current availability at your arrival point rather than assuming either card is always sold everywhere.
- **Deposit and refund process**: if you get a physical card, note that unused balance and the deposit are
  refundable in some cases before leaving Japan — check the current refund process at the station where you
  return it, since this has changed over time.

## Bottom line

Don't spend time deciding between Suica and Pasmo specifically — get whichever is available and convenient
where you land (or add either to your phone's wallet if supported), and focus your attention on how to
recharge and use it rather than which brand name is printed on it.
