Both an eSIM and a rented pocket WiFi router will get you online in Japan — the right choice depends less on
coverage quality (both are generally solid on Japan's networks) and more on how many people and devices need to
share the connection.

## eSIM: best for solo travelers and single devices

An eSIM installs directly into your phone with no extra hardware to carry, charge, or return at the end of the
trip. It only covers the one device it's installed on, which is exactly the point for a solo traveler — no
device to lose, nothing to hand back at the airport.

## Pocket WiFi: better for groups and multiple devices

A single pocket WiFi unit can connect several devices at once, which makes it noticeably cheaper per person
when traveling as a couple, family, or group compared to buying a separate eSIM for each phone. Japan-focused
rental services like Sakura Mobile offer pocket WiFi rental specifically set up for tourists, with English-
language support if something doesn't connect correctly after landing.

## The trade-offs of pocket WiFi

- **Another device to carry and keep charged** — most rental units last a full day on a charge, but it's one
  more thing to remember, unlike an eSIM which lives inside a phone you're already carrying.
- **Pickup/return logistics.** Airport counter pickup and return (or a return envelope) adds a small amount of
  process compared to an eSIM's instant app-based install.
- **Everyone needs to stay near the unit.** If your group splits up during the day, only whoever is carrying the
  router has a connection.

## Bottom line

Traveling solo or with your own separate data needs — an eSIM is simpler and nothing to carry. Traveling as a
couple, family, or group who'll mostly stay together during the day — a single pocket WiFi rental is usually
the cheaper, simpler option per person.
