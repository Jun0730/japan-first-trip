Both the Narita Express (N'EX) and the Keisei Skyliner run from Narita Airport into central Tokyo, but they
serve somewhat different routes and destinations — the right one depends mainly on where in Tokyo you're
headed.

## Narita Express (N'EX)

Runs directly to multiple major Tokyo stations without a transfer — including Tokyo Station, Shinjuku, Shibuya,
and Yokohama on certain routes — making it the more flexible option if your hotel is near one of these hub
stations. Generally a longer ride than the Skyliner but avoids a transfer for many destinations.

## Keisei Skyliner

Runs to Ueno and Nippori stations only, but does so faster than the N'EX on that specific route, since it's a
more direct high-speed line. The trade-off is that if your hotel isn't near Ueno or Nippori, you'll need an
additional transfer on the Tokyo subway/rail network from there.

## How to decide

- **Staying near Tokyo Station, Shinjuku, or Shibuya:** the Narita Express reaches these directly without a
  transfer, which usually outweighs its longer overall ride time.
- **Staying near Ueno, Asakusa, or northeastern Tokyo:** the Skyliner to Ueno/Nippori plus a short local transfer
  is often faster overall than the N'EX's more roundabout routing to those areas.
- **Traveling with a JR Pass already validated**: the N'EX is covered by the nationwide JR Pass in many cases,
  which can make it the lower-cost option if you already have one — the Skyliner is a separate private railway
  and isn't covered by the JR Pass.

## Bottom line

Pick based on your hotel's location and transfer tolerance rather than which train is generally "better" —
N'EX for direct access to major hub stations, Skyliner for speed if Ueno/Nippori and a short transfer work for
your destination.
