Nara was Japan's capital before Kyoto, and it's usually visited as a day trip from either Kyoto or Osaka rather
than as an overnight stop — both are about 45 minutes away by train, which makes Nara an easy half-day or
full-day add-on to a Kansai itinerary.

## What to expect

Nara Park is the main draw: hundreds of tame sika deer wander freely around the park and the temples inside it,
including Todai-ji, home to one of Japan's largest bronze Buddha statues. Deer here are used to visitors and
will approach for food, but they're still wild animals — feed them carefully with the crackers sold on-site and
avoid sudden movements.

## How much time to give Nara

A half-day (3-4 hours) covers Nara Park, Todai-ji and Kasuga Taisha. A full day lets you add Naramachi, the
old merchant quarter, for a quieter, less touristy contrast.

## Getting around

Everything most visitors want to see is walkable from Nara's two train stations, so you generally won't need
local transit within Nara itself.

## Suggested pairing

Most people day-trip to Nara from Kyoto in the morning and are back in time for dinner. It also works well as
a stop between Kyoto and Osaka if you're relocating between the two anyway.
