Kyoto was Japan's capital for over a thousand years, and it shows — this is the city most first-time visitors
picture when they imagine "traditional Japan." Wooden temples, quiet gardens, narrow lantern-lit streets, and a
food scene built around kaiseki and Buddhist vegetarian cooking all sit inside a modern, easy-to-navigate city.

## How much time to give Kyoto

Two to three full days is enough to see the highlights without rushing. If you only have one day, prioritize
Fushimi Inari early in the morning (before tour buses arrive) and the Higashiyama district in the afternoon.

## Getting around

Kyoto's bus network covers most tourist sites, but buses can get crowded and slow in peak season. For a first
visit, a mix of subway/train for longer hops and walking within districts like Higashiyama or Arashiyama tends
to work best. A day pass can be worth it if you're moving between several areas in one day — check current
options at Kyoto Station when you arrive, as passes and prices change.

## Where to base yourself

Kyoto Station is the most convenient base for first-timers: direct trains to Osaka and Nara, lockers for day
trips, and easy access to buses covering the rest of the city. See our [Kyoto Station area guide](/where-to-stay/kyoto-station-area/)
for specifics.
